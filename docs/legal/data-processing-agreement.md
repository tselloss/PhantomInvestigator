# Data Processing Agreement

**Effective date:** [DATE]
**Between:** [Company Legal Name] ("Company," "Processor") and the Client identified in the
applicable order form or account registration ("Client," "Controller")

This Data Processing Agreement ("DPA") supplements the [Terms of Service](./terms-of-service.md)
and applies whenever Company processes personal data on Client's behalf as part of providing
Phantom Investigator (the "Service") — specifically, personal data that may be incidentally
present in the Jira tickets, GitHub Actions data, and logs/metrics Client connects for
investigation (e.g., a customer's email address appearing in an error log line). Where this DPA
conflicts with the Terms of Service on data protection matters, this DPA controls.

## 1. Definitions

"Data Protection Laws" means all laws applicable to the processing of personal data under this
DPA, including, where applicable, the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and UK GDPR.
"Personal Data," "Processing," "Controller," "Processor," "Sub-processor," and "Data Subject"
have the meanings given in the GDPR, applied to other Data Protection Laws as relevant.
"Client Data" means personal data within the data Client connects to or submits through the
Service that Company processes on Client's behalf.

## 2. Roles of the Parties

Client is the Controller of Client Data. Company is a Processor, processing Client Data only on
Client's documented instructions as set out in this DPA and as generated by Client's use of the
Service (e.g., connecting an integration, triggering an investigation). Company does not
determine the purposes or means of processing Client Data beyond what is necessary to operate the
Service.

For the separate category of data covered by the [Privacy Policy](./privacy-policy.md) — the
account data of individuals who log into the Service's console — Company is the Controller. That
category is out of scope of this DPA.

## 3. Company's Obligations

Company will:

**3.1** Process Client Data only on Client's documented instructions, including with regard to
international transfers, unless required to do otherwise by law (in which case Company will
inform Client of that legal requirement first, unless the law prohibits doing so).

**3.2** Ensure persons authorized to process Client Data (Company personnel, and any
sub-processor's personnel) are subject to confidentiality obligations.

**3.3** Implement appropriate technical and organizational security measures under GDPR Article
32, having regard to the state of the art, costs, and the risk to data subjects. As implemented
in the Service today, these include:

- Encryption of stored integration credentials (API tokens, keys) using symmetric encryption
  at rest, decrypted only in-memory when needed to call the relevant integration.
- Password hashing with argon2; passwords are never stored or logged in plaintext.
- Per-account data isolation: each registered account's clients, deployments, investigations, and
  credentials are scoped to that account and inaccessible to others.
- Session authentication via signed, `HttpOnly`, `Secure` cookies.
- TLS in transit for the Service's public endpoints.
- A human-approval gate before the Service posts any content back to an external system (e.g.,
  Jira) — no automated write happens without an authenticated operator's review.
- [Add once finalized: backup encryption/frequency, access logging, vulnerability management
  cadence, incident response runbook.]

**3.4** Not engage a new sub-processor without giving Client prior notice and an opportunity to
object, per Section 6.

**3.5** Taking into account the nature of the processing, assist Client with responding to data
subject requests (access, correction, deletion, objection) concerning Client Data, to the extent
Company can identify the relevant data. Because Client Data may include personal data of Client's
own customers (data subjects Company has no direct relationship with), Client remains primarily
responsible for responding to those data subjects; Company will provide reasonable technical
assistance (e.g., search/export/delete a specific data subject's data from investigations and
traces on request).

**3.6** Assist Client, on request and at Client's reasonable expense for anything beyond
standard support, with data protection impact assessments and prior consultations with
supervisory authorities where required by Data Protection Laws.

**3.7** Notify Client without undue delay, and in any case within **48 hours** of becoming aware,
of any confirmed personal data breach affecting Client Data, providing the information reasonably
available at the time and updating it as the investigation progresses — so Client can meet its
own 72-hour regulatory notification obligation.

**3.8** At Client's choice, delete or return all Client Data at the end of the provision of
services relating to processing, and delete existing copies within [30] days, unless Data
Protection Laws or a legitimate business need (e.g., billing records) require Company to retain
it, in which case Company will continue to protect it under this DPA's terms.

**3.9** Make available to Client information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with
this Section 3, and allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, conducted by
Client or an auditor mandated by Client, with reasonable advance notice and no more than
[once per 12 months] absent a suspected breach — [may be satisfied via a third-party
certification/report instead of a full audit, if Company obtains one; confirm approach with
counsel/as the business matures].

## 4. Sub-processors

**4.1 General authorization.** Client authorizes Company to engage the sub-processors listed in
[Appendix A](#appendix-a-sub-processors) as of the effective date.

**4.2 Notice of new sub-processors.** Company will give Client at least [14] days' notice (e.g.,
by email or an update to this document) before engaging a new sub-processor for Client Data.
Client may object on reasonable data-protection grounds within that period; the parties will work
in good faith to resolve the objection, and if unresolved, Client may terminate the affected
portion of the Service without penalty.

**4.3** Company remains liable for a sub-processor's acts and omissions to the same extent Company
would be liable if performing that processing itself, and imposes data protection terms on each
sub-processor no less protective than this DPA.
